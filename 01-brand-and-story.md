# .Ricky — Brand & Story Brief

## Who

Swayam ("Ricky"), Senior Cloud / DevOps Engineer at TCS. This is a personal
brand site, not a company site. Audience: prospective clients and collaborators
for cloud architecture, security advisory, and bespoke web builds — but the
site is selling the person as much as the service.

## Who Ricky is

The job title is the least interesting thing here, and a brief that stops at
"DevOps engineer" will produce a site that looks like every other engineer's.

Ricky is a generalist by temperament, not by accident. Cloud architecture is
what he is paid for; it is not the boundary of what he does. He photographs.
He builds things outside his lane. He is drawn to work that is unlike the last
thing he made.

The traits that actually drive the design:

- **Free-willed.** Not contrarian, but genuinely unwilling to be boxed into one
  discipline or one way of doing things. Restless is the first value for a
  reason — it is descriptive, not aspirational.
- **Open.** Approachable, open to change, open to being wrong, open to a
  direction he did not think of. Curious about what is past the edge of what he
  already knows.
- **Out to make a difference.** Wants the work to matter and to be different
  from what is already there. "Different" is the operative word — a competent
  version of a familiar thing is a failure state.
- **A photographer's eye.** This is the single most useful fact for anyone
  making visual decisions. He thinks in frames, light, and composition rather
  than in layouts and components.

### What this means for design decisions

- **The site is a camera move, not a page.** That is not a gimmick chosen for
  novelty — it comes directly from how he sees. Preserve it in any treatment.
- **Light does the storytelling.** Across the whole journey, the emotional
  register is carried by where the light is and how much of it there is:
  a slot of sky, a distant aperture, a gold horizon, a city glow. Get the light
  right and the composition follows.
- **Nothing templated.** A design that reads as a good default is a wrong
  answer regardless of how well it is executed. This has already killed one
  direction in this project.
- **Restraint, not minimalism for its own sake.** The world is spare because
  spare frames read better and compress better, not because minimal is a style
  goal. Detail is welcome where it earns its place.
- **Approachability matters in the copy.** The tone is direct and human, not
  corporate and not clever. "Got something worth unzipping?" is the register.

## The journey

One continuous forward camera move through a single connected world. The
reader's scroll is the transport — nothing plays on its own, and scrolling
back runs the journey in reverse. There are no cuts anywhere in the piece.

1. **Canyon** — narrow slot canyon, cool blue-grey sculpted sandstone, a bright
   slot of sky above. Carries Restless.
2. **The fork** — the canyon splits; the camera passes one side. Carries
   Instinct. This is a deliberate beat and needs scroll room to land.
3. **Cave and stream** — the corridor darkens, water underfoot, a small warm
   aperture appears far ahead and grows as you approach. Carries Curiosity.
4. **Emergence** — out of the cave mouth onto open water at dusk. Gold light
   along the horizon. Walls fall away behind. Fog. Carries Kinetic.
5. **The city** — fog thins, a coastal skyline resolves on the horizon, warm
   lights diffused by residual haze. Carries Yes.

## The closing beat

This is coded, never generated. It must survive any change of visual treatment.

The gold horizon glow contracts down to a single dot. The dot rises. The
wordmark `.ricky` resolves around it, with the dot landing as the leading
period. Subtitle *Unzip the Vision* fades in beneath.

The contraction must start exactly where the glow actually sits in the final
frame — in the current build that is x 49.7%, y 51.2%. Measure it; do not
assume centre.

## Standing constraints

- **No degraded loading.** Never show a low-resolution or blurred pass. A
  visitor who scrolls past bad quality does not come back. Hold on the last
  sharp state rather than showing a soft one.
- **No cuts.** Every transition between beats must be continuous motion.
- **Watermark.** The Veo/Gemini sparkle mark is present in all generated
  footage, bottom-right, centred at 91.5% across and 83.3% down, about 3.2% of
  frame width. It stays. Do not crop it out, cover it, or place UI over it.
